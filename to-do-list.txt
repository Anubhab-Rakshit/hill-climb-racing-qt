Phase 4 — Terrain topology overhaul
This is the highest-priority phase.
Replace the single sine formula with a data-driven track system:
- Add TrackRecipe / TerrainGenerator: deterministic seed, difficulty curve, ordered sections, and biome-specific feature recipes.
- Add multi-octave value noise, ridged noise, domain warping, and roughness layers at 0.5 m / 2 m / 8 m scales.
- Add a real feature library:
- rollers, whoops, washboard bumps
- tabletops, doubles, step-ups, drop-offs
- ledges, cliffs, ravines, ridges, plateaus
- sand dune crests, ice shelves, rock steps, ash slopes
- Blend features with C1-continuous entry/exit transitions so there are no accidental kinks.
- Keep the first 20 m flat, then immediately introduce the biome’s signature terrain.
- Extend the validator: max climbable slope, safe jump landing angles, fuel reachability, no long boring flat sections, checkpoint spacing.
Done when: all 9 maps have distinct topology and signature sections, and automated tests prove every generated map is traversable.
Phase 5 — Surface material physics
This fixes “different terrain should have different physics.”
Add a proper material system:
- SurfaceMaterial table: grass, dirt, mud, sand, gravel, rock, ice, packed snow, ash, regolith, tarmac, neon.
- Each material gets its own friction, rolling resistance, sinkage/drag, deformation rate, restitution, dust particle, and tire audio.
- Terrain::getSurfaceAt(x) returns the local material, with blended transition zones.
- Box2D collider strips become material-aware, so each 0.5 m strip can have different friction.
- Legacy solver samples material at each wheel contact instead of using one biome-wide value.
- Per-biome material maps, e.g. desert = sand + rock outcrops, arctic = ice + snow + rock, mountain = rock + scree, volcano = basalt + ash, seasons = grass → dry dirt → mud → ice.
Done when: the same slope, same vehicle, and same input produce clearly different acceleration, wheelspin, braking distance, and slide behavior on sand, ice, mud, rock, and grass.
Phase 6 — Vehicle dynamics realism
- Add a real engine torque curve, RPM/load behavior, engine braking, and drivetrain split.
- Improve tires: slip ratio, load-sensitive grip, combined slip, rolling resistance, wheel sinkage.
- Improve suspension: progressive springs, bump stops, compression/rebound damping, unsprung mass.
- Add aero drag and stronger downforce effects.
- Tune all 12 vehicles so they feel genuinely different on the new terrain.
- Keep Box2D as the backend; do not restart the physics work.
Done when: each vehicle has a distinct acceleration, braking, climbing, and landing character, and none of them become unstable on rough terrain.
Phase 7 — Driver ragdoll, damage, and crashes
- Replace the head-spring with a multi-body driver ragdoll: torso, head, arms/legs, seat belt, neck limits.
- Add ejection on severe crashes, helmet/body collisions, and a proper “driver down” slow-motion moment.
- Add damage zones: front, rear, underbody, wheels, suspension.
- Damage affects performance: bent wheels, engine smoke, suspension sag, reduced power.
- Add crash camera, impact audio, smoke/fire particles, and repair/restart flow.
Done when: bad landings and rollovers look and feel dangerous, not just trigger an instant game-over text.
Phase 8 — Map identity, visuals, and weather
- Material-specific terrain rendering: sand ripples, snow caps, ice cracks, rock striations, mud, ash, lava veins, neon grid.
- Better cliff faces, strata, ledges, and surface detail.
- Tire tracks, skid marks, dust/snow/rain/ash weather, improved lighting and shadows.
- More biome-specific landmarks and set pieces at checkpoints.
- Keep everything inside the software rasterizer; no QPainter vector shapes.
Done when: every map is recognizable from a single screenshot, not just from its color palette.
Phase 9 — Game feel, audio, camera, HUD, progression
- Dynamic engine audio tied to RPM/load, surface-specific tire loops, impacts, wind.
- Camera anticipation, landing zoom, crash slow-motion, better shake.
- HUD additions: RPM/gear, surface indicator, damage/fuel status, better terrain-profile minimap.
- Make upgrades actually affect the new physics: engine, tires, suspension, 4WD, brakes.
- Add challenge modes, ghosts/replays, and better reward pacing.
Done when: driving feels responsive and readable, and upgrades are felt immediately.
Phase 10 — Content, balance, multiplayer, performance, release
- Finish and balance all 9 biomes and 12 vehicles.
- Sync terrain seeds, materials, ragdolls, and damage in multiplayer.
- Performance pass for 60 FPS at 960×540 and 1280×720.
- Final QA, screenshots, docs, packaging, and release.
Recommended order
Do Phase 4 + Phase 5 together as one “Terrain & Surface Realism Sprint.” They depend on each other: the material system must be designed into the generator and collider, not bolted on afterward.
